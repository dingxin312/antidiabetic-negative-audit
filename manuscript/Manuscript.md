# Negative evidence in AI-assisted discovery of food-derived antidiabetic peptides: Construction, benchmark reuse and validation boundaries

## Abstract

**Background:** A negative label in antidiabetic-peptide prediction can represent measured non-response, missing activity annotation or a constructed comparator. The distinction matters when a predictor is used to select peptides from food proteins.

**Scope and approach:** We synthesize methodological studies and published food-peptide experiments alongside a sequence-level audit of 21 purposively selected prediction studies. We distinguish the groups defined by training labels, the comparison measured by an evaluation procedure and the response established by a controlled food experiment.

**Key findings and conclusions:** Negative sequences were retrievable for 17 of the 21 studies. Seven of these accessible versions share all 665 negative identities and the same 532/133 train/test membership. Their deposited files assign opposite labels to seven identities, all of which match a bovine beta-casein reference sequence; a length-filtered input retains them, whereas a reported re-curation removes them. In a second reference set, earlier test sequences acquire development or external-test roles. Two examined implementations further show how file routing and test-guided selection affect the interpretation of internal scores. Published food experiments show that confirming selected peptides, estimating potency and measuring an added effect in a processed matrix require different observations. These findings delimit the uses of a benchmark score; they neither invalidate separately controlled food experiments nor estimate field-wide performance effects.

**Keywords:** food-derived peptides; antidiabetic activity; negative samples; benchmark provenance; label uncertainty; validation

# 1. Introduction

Food proteins supply a large peptide sequence space through enzymatic hydrolysis, fermentation and digestion. Computational screening can narrow the candidates for synthesis and testing, while laboratory studies must still identify whether an observed activity belongs to an intact protein, a hydrolysate, a fraction or an individual peptide. Reviews have described the data, representation, model and validation workflow and its applications to food-peptide discovery (Chang et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2026). In practice, BERT-DPPIV guided synthesis and inhibition measurements, machine-learning and virtual-proteolysis strategies identified bovine-milk candidates, and a duckweed study combined peptide profiling with modelling and tests of several activities (Guan et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024; Cournoyer et al., 2025). These examples put a specific question at the centre of AI-assisted discovery: the evidence that connects a model's predicted category to the food-derived material and endpoint that researchers actually test.

The negative class is a particularly consequential point of translation. A peptide below an experimental activity threshold, a sequence absent from an activity database, an antiviral peptide used as a comparator and a randomly generated sequence may all receive a zero in a training file. AntiDMPpred describes its antiviral-source negatives as having no reported antidiabetic activity, while ADP-Fuse combines random and other-function peptides (Chen et al., 2022; Basith et al., 2023; Qureshi et al., 2014). Positive-unlabelled learning formalizes why a missing target annotation does not establish biological inactivity (Elkan and Noto, 2008). Constructed comparators can still improve a modelling task: stochastic negative addition helped one molecular-screening benchmark but reduced performance on a temporal benchmark (Cáceres et al., 2020). The question is which contrast the sampling rule creates and what decision that contrast can inform.

The surrounding literature addresses different parts of this problem. Chang et al. organize AI-assisted food-peptide screening by workflow and application; Zhao et al. examine methodological limitations (Chang et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2026). Luo et al. investigate test conditions and separate functional outputs in peptide models, Yang et al. discuss model choice in relation to food-matrix data and deployment tasks, and Alaei et al. examine the evidence needed for operational claims in food processing (Luo et al., 2027; Yang et al., 2026; Alaei et al., 2026). The ADP-Hybrid preprint reconstructs an antidiabetic benchmark under homology-aware splits and audits fragment-label patterns in 16 further peptide datasets (Islam et al., 2026, preprint). Here the unit of analysis is the released negative-set version: its sequence members, conflicting labels and changing evaluation roles across selected predictors. We relate those observations to the specific conclusions supported by published food-peptide experiments, without treating a model score and an assay response as the same kind of evidence.

DOME and REFORMS distinguish data provenance, optimization decisions and evaluation design, and peptide benchmarks show why sampling and partitioning deserve scrutiny alongside architecture (Walsh et al., 2021; Kapoor et al., 2024; Sidorczuk et al., 2022). Our synthesis has three analytical levels (Figure 1): training labels define the groups learned by a model; evaluation decisions define what its reported score measures; and food experiments supply observations and controls for an intended use. Sections 3 and 4 examine the first two levels, while Section 5 examines the third. Table 1 distinguishes sequence-level negative labels from experimental food controls; evaluation roles are examined in Table 2 and Section 4. A benchmark weakness limits what its score can establish, while an independently controlled food experiment is judged by its own measured outcome.

# 2. Review scope and evidence approach

This review combines focused critical synthesis with a bounded provenance audit. We assembled the supporting literature from previously collected food-peptide and modelling papers and their cited primary sources. Sources were retained when they clarified label meaning, negative sampling, evaluation design or an observed food-screening comparison; food examples cover candidate selection, assay and material context, and processing or matrix effects. This is a purposeful selection rather than a systematic survey. The literature and model-audit cutoff was 2 November 2026; later issue years refer to work already available online, and preprints are identified. Supplementary Table S1 records each supporting source and the observation used here.

The primary sample comprised 20 previously identified sequence-based DPP-IV or antidiabetic-peptide predictor records, A1 to A14 and H1 to H6. Bilingua-DPP-IV (B1) was added after that sample had been set to examine a documented re-curation and external evaluation. This convenience sample is not a census of peptide predictors. All 21 records are mapped to their tasks in Supplementary Table S3 and to source artifacts in Supplementary Table S2; included records remained in the denominator when their negative sequences were inaccessible. General antidiabetic activity, DPP-IV inhibition, diabetes-type classification and residue-level prediction were treated as distinct targets (Basith et al., 2023; Cai et al., 2024; Basith et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2026, preprint). Sequence comparisons use identified accessible versions, including an archived A3 copy, author-repository A5 files, the H6 model input and a pinned B1 implementation. The food studies in Section 5 supply evidence about application to food-derived candidates, regardless of whether their predictor was in the audit sample.

We recorded three audit dimensions: completeness of an identified negative-sequence version, its relationship to a reference set and its negative split identity (Table 2; Supplementary Table S3). Completeness means that the version's full negative membership was obtained; partial covers an incomplete version or unresolved paper-to-file correspondence; and not verified covers source links, unavailable files and noncomparable tasks. Relationship records exact identity, containment, filtering or re-curation relative to F665 or F966, designations for sequence reference sets. Split identity compares accessible training and test membership with H1 or records an own split, development pool or incomplete partition. H6's full 629-member input is thus complete even though it is a filtered F665 subset with its own split; A9's 899 development negatives belong to a 1,798-record positive-plus-negative pool, with the reported internal 80/20 division not recovered (Balachandran and Bhadra, 2026; Xie et al., 2025). A6 remains source-linked to A2 without a verified independent deposit (Arshad et al., 2024).

We retained original sequences and source locators alongside normalized sequences, used uppercase and whitespace removal, and removed terminal `X` padding only where A7 documented that representation. Record counts, unique identities, opposite labels and train/test intersections were calculated separately. We reconstructed H6 partitions under specified random seeds and inspected code for two mechanism examples: A4 exposes a validation/test loader route, while B1 exposes ensemble-selection calls. This was not a systematic code audit of the other 19 records, and no predictor was retrained. Software versions, input hashes, code locations and detailed reconstruction steps are in the Supplementary Material.

For a bounded food-source annotation, we matched the archived A3 copy of the F665 positive and negative files against six reviewed bovine-milk protein sequences from UniProt (The UniProt Consortium, 2025). This additional reference lookup was frozen on 5 November 2026. Exact contiguous matches were counted for distinct sequences in each class, with a minimum length of five residues to reduce incidental short-peptide matches; a six-residue threshold and length strata provide sensitivity checks. The seven cross-label identities occur once in each class and are reported separately from per-class totals. The frozen protein FASTA, its retrieval manifest, four A3 input hashes, per-sequence matches and offline recomputation script accompany Supplementary Table S8.

# 3. What a negative label means in food-peptide prediction

## 3.1 What activity resources record

Food-peptide databases organize observations and annotations rather than a complete inventory of active and inactive sequences. BIOPEP-UWM links peptides to activities and bibliographic sources and distinguishes measured potency from records without a numerical value (Minkiewicz et al., 2019). DFBP reports 6,276 activity entries representing 3,997 distinct sequences and supports multifunctional analysis and tracing peptides to food proteins (Qin et al., 2022). When these records enter a prediction dataset, the unit of analysis matters: activity entries can duplicate a sequence, and absence from a functional category may reflect the scope of testing. The measured function and its experimental conditions must therefore travel with the source record. That provenance determines whether a missing target annotation is an unlabelled observation or a defensible source for a particular comparator group.

Specialized resources provide different parts of that provenance. The Milk Bioactive Peptide Database focuses on experimentally reported milk-protein peptides, BioPepDB integrates food-derived bioactive-peptide information, and FermFooDb connects fermented-food sequences to food source, fermentation conditions and testing models (Nielsen et al., 2017; Li et al., 2018; Chaudhary et al., 2021). A milk entry can link a sequence to a precursor and an activity study; a fermentation record can locate the process that yielded it. These fields support source-aware dataset construction, while unlisted peptide-endpoint combinations remain unmeasured unless the underlying experiments say otherwise. Researchers can represent that missing evidence through a declared sampling convention, an unlabelled-data approach or targeted additional testing. The choice changes what the trained model learns to distinguish and should be made with the screening population in view.

Sequence provenance and functional evidence also answer different questions. BioDADPep assembles peptides associated with type 1 and type 2 diabetes and their targets; UniProt can help identify candidate protein precursors (Roy and Teron, 2019; The UniProt Consortium, 2025). ADP-Fuse (A2) first classifies ADPs versus non-ADPs and then separates diabetes-type labels, whereas the T1DM/T2DM classifier (A8) uses disease categories directly (Basith et al., 2023; Cai et al., 2024). A zero in a diabetes-type task can designate the other disease category, with a meaning quite different from an experimentally inactive DPP-IV peptide. Similarly, finding a sequence within a food protein establishes a possible precursor, while release and concentration depend on processing and digestion. Keeping disease association, precursor identity and measured inhibition as separate attributes prevents a single binary label from obscuring the task.

The F665 files provide a concrete test of what precursor annotation adds. In the archived StackDPPIV copy, the 665 unique positive and 665 unique negative sequences have median lengths of four and 15 residues; 352 positives, but no negatives, have at most four residues. Among sequences of at least five residues, 198 of 313 positives and 59 of 665 negatives occur as exact substrings of six selected bovine-milk reference proteins. All seven identities assigned opposing labels in these files match the beta-casein reference P02666. The two classes share those seven identities, so their counts are not disjoint. These are reference-sequence matches and a length contrast within this benchmark, not determinations of the peptides' experimental origins or of which features a predictor learned (Supplementary Table S8).

## 3.2 Other-function, random and null comparators answer different questions

Other-function peptides provide experimentally studied comparator sequences. AVPdb records antiviral peptides, DRAMP antimicrobial peptides, and SATPdb and THPdb broader therapeutic sequences or approved peptide and protein products (Qureshi et al., 2014; Singh et al., 2016; Usmani et al., 2017; Kang et al., 2019). AntiDMPpred draws negatives from AVPdb and describes their antidiabetic activity as unreported (Chen et al., 2022). Bizzotto et al. instead build one-versus-rest tasks from other annotated functional groups (Bizzotto et al., 2024). Both are interpretable classification contrasts when the sampling rule is declared. They ask whether a model separates the selected annotation groups; an assay of target-enzyme inhibition asks a different question about a particular peptide's response.

Multifunctionality makes the distinction observable. Cournoyer et al. tested duckweed peptides across DPP-IV inhibition, ACE inhibition and antioxidant assays, and DFBP accommodates multiple activities for one sequence (Cournoyer et al., 2025; Qin et al., 2022). An other-function label carries positive evidence for its recorded function while leaving target activity to be measured. Positive-unlabelled learning distinguishes an unrecorded class label from an observed negative, although its application depends on how known positives were selected (Elkan and Noto, 2008). In food-peptide screening, an other-function comparator can support separation of sampled groups; establishing target-specific inhibition still requires a target-specific observation.

Randomly sampled and generated controls define a contrast through their construction rule. iDPPIV-SCM (H1) describes random unique peptides alongside functional non-inhibitory wording, while ADP-Fuse (A2) combines random and other-function components (Charoenkwan et al., 2020; Basith et al., 2023). In molecular screening, stochastic addition of untested molecule-target pairs improved one benchmark but slightly reduced performance on a temporal benchmark (Cáceres et al., 2020). Antimicrobial-peptide benchmarks likewise respond to the negative-sampling procedure and to how training and evaluation negatives relate (Sidorczuk et al., 2022). The modelling value of constructed controls therefore depends on the task and evaluation population. Stating how they were sampled gives a score an interpretable reference without presenting an untested sequence as a measured biological non-responder.

The term “null” also describes an assigned modelling role. The accessible A12 spreadsheet contains 200 synthetic null peptides, with correspondence to the paper's larger training and evaluation partitions unresolved (Rahmani Asl et al., 2026). Their construction rule, rather than an individual DPP-IV assay, is the available evidence for those records. Sequence length, residue composition, source restrictions and separation from the positives determine the contrast a generated comparator presents to a classifier. F665's observed length distribution gives a specific reason to compare length-aware baselines and length-matched candidate sets before attributing a benchmark result to a learned inhibition pattern. The sampling domain also determines how directly a score addresses the peptides among which a food researcher must choose.

## 3.3 Experimental inactivity is endpoint- and condition-specific

An experimental non-response is defined by its endpoint, concentration range, decision criterion, assay sensitivity and controls. The milk-peptide QSAR study shows how assay differences complicate the combination of measured potencies (Nongonierma and FitzGerald, 2016); Section 5.2 examines ranking and numerical prediction. Peptide identity and sample composition can also change during measurement. For Leu-Pro-Leu-Pro-Leu, Nongonierma and FitzGerald report an IC50 of 358.4 ± 15.6 μM in the control and 246.7 ± 66.5 μM after incubation with DPP-IV at their high enzyme-to-substrate ratio; the latter measures the treated material, which contains cleavage products (Nongonierma and FitzGerald, 2014, Table 3). Substrate-type, prodrug-type and true-inhibitor behaviour therefore describe different relationships between the starting peptide and the tested material. A binary activity label that loses its assay conditions cannot retain this distinction. Opposing labels for one sequence are an observable inconsistency in a sequence-only benchmark, even when their biological histories have yet to be examined.

Table 1 groups the sources of negative labels by their observed evidence: missing target annotation, activity for another function, a generation rule or a measured non-response. A sequence can carry more than one of these attributes, and the food control in the final row is an experimental condition rather than another kind of peptide label. With those objects distinguished, Section 4 examines how membership, partitioning and model selection define the comparison represented by a reported benchmark score.

# 4. How construction, reuse and evaluation define the tested comparison

## 4.1 The sampled contrast can differ from the intended biological task

A negative-sampling procedure determines the contrast available to a classifier. Differences in source, length, composition and sequence families can separate positive and negative files alongside, or instead of, the intended functional target. Shortcut-learning research explains how a rule can perform well within one collection and fail when its collection-specific cues change; systematic biological audits use controlled comparisons to identify the signal actually learned (Geirhos et al., 2020; Eid et al., 2021). Peptide properties may themselves be biologically relevant, so their presence as predictors is informative only in relation to the task and sampling design. Sidorczuk et al. directly vary negative sampling in antimicrobial prediction and find that benchmark results depend on the relationship between training and evaluation negatives (Sidorczuk et al., 2022). For antidiabetic screening, this points to a concrete evaluation question: whether the tested comparator distribution resembles the candidates among which a decision will be made.

The ADP-Hybrid preprint examines source attribution, length baselines and homology-aware partitions in ADP-Fuse (Islam et al., 2026, preprint). Candidate precursors are assigned to 218 of 877 positives: 140 human-insulin fragments carry the type-1 label, 76 bovine-milk-protein fragments the type-2 label, and two remaining fragments are human amylin. Its audit of 16 other peptide benchmarks finds excess within-family label sharing in 11 of 14 testable datasets. In the reconstructed ADP-Fuse comparison, an architectural advantage seen under random partitioning disappears under a homology-aware split. These are the preprint's reconstructed performance results, whereas our F665 source annotation in Section 3.1 and the version audit below examine deposited members and roles without fitting a predictor. Both lines of evidence make the source and length distribution part of the comparison a score must be interpreted against.

Simple-feature baselines offer a way to examine which information a sampled task rewards. Bizzotto et al. compare encodings and classifiers across nine peptide function groups with differing sizes and sequence properties; Adamczyk et al. report strong results for local molecular fingerprints across multiple peptide datasets (Bizzotto et al., 2024; Adamczyk et al., 2026). A length or composition baseline measures how readily the collection can be separated using such features. Comparing a more elaborate model against that baseline under source- or homology-aware partitions then tests whether its advantage persists when dependencies change. Together with negative-sampling experiments, these studies connect model comparison to the candidate population and the specific distribution of the comparator class (Sidorczuk et al., 2022).

## 4.2 Exact benchmark reuse and version relationships

The audit resolves several version relationships at the level of sequence identity (Figure 2). Negative sequences were retrievable for 17 of 21 study records, with 14 complete for their identified version; within the 20 primary records, the counts were 16/20 and 13/20. The remaining statuses comprise three partial or unresolved records and four source-linked, unavailable or noncomparable records (A6, A8, A10 and A11; Supplementary Table S3). Seven audited versions, including the archive-recovered StackDPPIV (A3) and author-repository BERT-DPPIV (A5) copies, contain the same 665 negative identities and the same 532/133 train/test negative members: iDPPIV-SCM (H1), iDPPIV-SI (H2), iDPPIV-PC (H3), IPPF-FE (H5), A3, StructuralDPPIV (A4) and A5 (Charoenkwan et al., 2020; Zou, 2024; Zou and Yin, 2021; Yu and Luo, 2023; Charoenkwan et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024; Guan et al., 2023). Several authors acknowledge reuse. These versions can compare algorithms on the same reference cohort, but agreement among their scores is not replication on seven independent test cohorts. A4's paper and accessible test file also report different counts, as examined in Section 4.5.

Two related versions separate membership from partition identity (Table 2). SeqDPPIV (H6) supplies its complete 629-negative input, precisely the F665 members of at most 33 residues, and makes its own random split (Balachandran and Bhadra, 2026). BBATProt (H4) provides 532 training negatives identical to H1/H3, but its reported 133 test negatives were not available in the inspected release (Wang et al., 2025). The H3 test file remains a possible source, not a verified H4 test partition.

Yue et al. (A7) provide 4,200 training and 1,050 test negatives in the repository, while the paper describes retaining 3,970 of an initial 4,000 training negatives; that mapping remains unresolved (Yue et al., 2024). The repository documents terminal `X` padding on 4,189 training negatives. After its removal, the repository sequences intersect F665 by 51 identities, F966 by 101, the full ADP-Fuse (A2) set by 120 and AntiDMPpred (A1) by 205 (Figure 3). These are exact intersections for the normalized repository version; near-homology and shared precursors are separate questions (Whalen et al., 2022).

## 4.3 Opposite labels retained and removed across versions

The seven fixed-partition F665 versions share seven sequences with opposing labels in the deposited files. Four, GPFPIIV, LHLPLP, LPVPQ and LTQTPVVVPPF, occur in both training classes. Three also cross the fixed train/test boundary with opposing labels: FAQTQS is a training negative and test positive, whereas LTDVEN and VRGPFP are training positives and test negatives (Table 3). Thus, seven of the 665 negative identities (1.1%) conflict with a positive identity, and three have an occurrence among the 266 test records. Their presence in seven inspected versions produces 49 sequence-by-version records, not 49 distinct errors. All seven match bovine beta-casein in the bounded annotation reported in Section 3.1. The deposited labels are inconsistent for a sequence-only target; the original activity records are needed to interpret their biological status.

Filtering and re-curation affect these identities differently. The SeqDPPIV (H6) length-filtered input retains all seven but uses its own split (Balachandran and Bhadra, 2026); reconstructed split results are supplied in the Supplementary Material. The accessible Bilingua-DPP-IV (B1) training, internal-test and external files exclude all seven from both classes (Zhang et al., 2026). Its benchmark negatives contain 658 retained F665 identities and three additions, with five further negatives in a separate external panel. The seven F665 negatives excluded from its benchmark are precisely the conflict identities, consistent with B1's reported re-curation and its own discussion of earlier label conflicts.

Removing contradictory identities makes a sequence-only benchmark internally more consistent; it leaves the activity status of the other negative members to be established from their source assays. The F665/B1 comparison identifies exactly which sequence members changed and which did not, allowing any comparison of benchmark versions to state its target. DOME's emphasis on sources and redundancy and the traceable records in food-peptide databases provide a way to document such changes (Walsh et al., 2021; Minkiewicz et al., 2019; Qin et al., 2022). These observed member changes are meaningful without attributing any difference in reported model scores to re-curation.

## 4.4 Source reuse across development and evaluation roles

The ADP-Fuse, BertADP and FedAntiDiaPep relationship (A2, A9 and A13) shows how shared identities can acquire new evaluation roles. ADP-Fuse (A2) and the DQN ensemble (A14) share an accessible 1,399-negative Layer-1 set with the same 658/741 training/test members (Basith et al., 2023; Kumar and Singh, 2026). BertADP (A9) uses all 658 A2 training negatives and 308 A2 test negatives: 241 of the latter enter A9 development and 67 enter its external set, leaving 433 A2 test negatives unused (Xie et al., 2025). Its development pool comprises 899 negatives and 899 positives, or 1,798 records, before the internal 80/20 division reported in the paper; the division's members were not recovered. Of the A2 test positives, 219 enter the A9 development pool and none enter its external panel (Supplementary Table S4). FedAntiDiaPep (A13) publishes 899-negative training and 67-negative test files that match A9's development and external negatives, respectively (Shoyshob et al., 2026).

A9 discloses its use of the earlier test pool, and its own development and external sets have no exact sequence intersection even when both labels are combined (Xie et al., 2025). “External” describes A9's within-study partition, while Supplementary Table S4 shows which of those identities had been used as A2 test members. Table 2 describes the released roles in each version. Source reuse and information use are distinct evaluation questions: sequence files establish the former, while judging the latter requires evidence about the decisions made after seeing evaluation outcomes (Kapoor and Narayanan, 2023; Walsh et al., 2021). The two implementation examples in Section 4.5 show why that further evidence matters.

## 4.5 From sample separation to decision separation

A test set's identity and its use in decision-making are separate properties of an evaluation. DOME and REFORMS include preprocessing, optimization and model selection in the procedure being assessed (Walsh et al., 2021; Kapoor et al., 2024). Cawley and Talbot explain how selecting a model against an evaluation criterion can itself overfit that criterion (Cawley and Talbot, 2010). Labels used to choose a checkpoint, threshold or ensemble can therefore influence the reported result even when the chosen predictor was fitted on a separate training file. Leakage reviews describe the wider range of such information flows (Kapoor and Narayanan, 2023). Inspecting the data path and selection calls makes this question testable in accessible implementations.

In the released StructuralDPPIV (A4) code pinned in Supplementary Table S2, the DPP-IV loader maps `valid` to `test`. Isolated reader calls return the same 266 ordered records, 133 positives and 133 negatives, for both roles. The training entry point uses validation accuracy to select a checkpoint and validation F1 for early stopping, then tests the selected checkpoint on those records (released code, `main/train.py`, lines 38 to 44). The inspected path therefore uses the accessible test file for both selection and testing. Wang et al. (2024), however, report 113 test examples per class; the connection between that report, the 133-per-class file and historical runs is unresolved. The paper/file discrepancy prevents attributing this code path to every published score.

The released Bilingua-DPP-IV (B1) code pinned in Supplementary Table S2 uses internal test outcomes for stacking, threshold optimization and ensemble-method selection (Zhang et al., 2026). In the stacking branch, a meta-model fits test labels, which the authors' own code warns is prone to leakage; the optimized branch uses test MCC to set a threshold, and the method-comparison block selects its winner by test MCC (`CNN_bimodal_Final.py`, lines 555 to 575 and 643 to 672). The paper also describes comparing ensemble strategies on internal test data before choosing the reported average strategy. The final `avg` predictor is distinct from the stacking fit, although its selection uses internal test results. An external panel of 44 positives and five negatives has no exact overlap with the accessible internal data and represents a different information boundary. Together, A4 and B1 illustrate why data-file separation and decisions made with the files must both be inspected; these code paths do not establish the magnitude of any published score difference.

# 5. What food experiments establish for screening

Food-peptide use raises three experimental questions within the third analytical level set out in the Introduction. Candidate selection determines which molecules are observed; assay and material choices determine the measured response; and processing or matrix controls determine the incremental effect relevant to a food use. Published experiments provide comparisons for these different claims. Their measured outcomes stand on their own even when the predictor used to shortlist candidates has an incompletely documented benchmark.

## 5.1 Candidate confirmation and screening performance

Candidate testing can establish a valuable discovery result. BERT-DPPIV reports synthesis and inhibition testing of selected tripeptides and pentapeptides; studies combining machine learning and virtual proteolysis identify and test bovine-milk and rice-protein candidates (Guan et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024; Cheng et al., 2025). The assays measure the selected molecules under their stated conditions. Estimating how well the predictor sorts an entire screening population calls for a different sampling design: outcomes are needed across the prediction groups, including candidates the pipeline would otherwise discard. When docking or manual filters are added after classifier scoring, the tested shortlist reflects the combined pipeline. Distinguishing discovery yield from classification error follows from specifying both the decision rule and the population on which it is evaluated (Walsh et al., 2021; Cawley and Talbot, 2010).

BERT-DPPIV (A5) links these evidence types in one published study. Its author-repository negative files have the same F665 train/test members as six other inspected versions, while Guan et al. (2023) separately synthesized tripeptides and pentapeptides and measured their DPP-IV inhibition. The measurements establish responses for the tested molecules. The shared benchmark identifies the reference population against which the model was scored; that score, even alongside successful candidate assays, does not measure how often the selection rule would succeed among all food peptides that might have been considered. A5 therefore makes the audit relevant to how a selection claim is evaluated without calling the reported molecular measurements into question.

The comparator in a candidate experiment determines which part of the selection rule it evaluates. A reference inhibitor checks assay responsiveness, a substrate-free blank establishes background, and a sampled low-score peptide tests a candidate that the predictor would otherwise deprioritize. Rice-peptide screening combines model scores, docking and simulation before assays on a selected set; goat-milk, oyster and pumpkin-seed studies likewise test prioritized candidates (Cheng et al., 2025; M. Liu et al., 2026; Dang et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2026). These assays can identify active selected molecules. Estimating enrichment or missed activity would also require a defined sampling frame and measurements among candidates outside the shortlist. Duckweed profiling and synthetic-peptide tests span additional activity endpoints, while quantification in a red-alga digest addresses occurrence in the tested material (Cournoyer et al., 2025; Kumagai et al., 2026).

## 5.2 Endpoint, material and assay context change the meaning of a comparison

Ranking candidates and estimating their numerical potency require different comparisons. Nongonierma and FitzGerald synthesized peptides from a set reported in the human intestinal tract and compared predicted with measured DPP-IV inhibition (Nongonierma and FitzGerald, 2016). Their ranking of competitive inhibitors was broadly conserved, while absolute predicted IC50 values disagreed with measurements: one descriptor model tended to underestimate and another to overestimate. This supports prioritization by order under the studied conditions, while using predicted concentrations calls for direct error assessment. The authors also document variation in assay conditions across reported potency values. A binary activity threshold retains the context of its source assay, including the enzyme preparation, protocol and decision threshold.

Within-study comparisons make changes in endpoint visible. For the chickpea-derived peptide IAIPPGIPYW, Zan et al. report an enzyme-assay IC50 of 12.43 ± 0.48 μM and a Caco-2-cell result of 138.63 ± 14.98 μM; PPGIPYW likewise differs between their enzyme and cell assays (Zan et al., 2023). The numerical difference reflects measurements in different experimental systems. Goat-milk peptides GPFPLL and LPYPY have also been examined through purified-peptide inhibition, digestion-related testing and a cellular experiment (Dang et al., 2025). Together these studies show what an additional experimental layer contributes: a new response under a new set of conditions. A model label constructed from enzyme measurements should retain that source, so a subsequent cellular measurement can be interpreted as evidence about translation rather than another estimate of the same quantity.

The material assayed also determines the unit of evidence. A mass-based IC50 for a hydrolysate measures a mixture; a molar IC50 for a synthetic peptide measures a defined molecular species. The black-bean study reports inhibition by hydrolysate fractions alongside candidate-sequence analyses (Ramirez-Lozano et al., 2025). Probiotic-fermented milk comparisons similarly involve products with multiple peptides and other constituents (Mudgil et al., 2024). Fraction-level activity can support development of a functional ingredient, while identifying the active sequence within that fraction calls for direct attribution. Recording whether an observation concerns a sequence, fraction, hydrolysate or complete food matrix allows experimental controls to match the material tested. It also prevents a mixture response from being assigned to a computationally prioritized constituent solely because that constituent could occur in the mixture.

A controlled material comparison can show that one processing change affects endpoints differently. Tenenbaum et al. compared whey protein isolate and hydrolysate after simulated gastrointestinal digestion, measuring recombinant and cellular DPP-IV responses, GLP-1 secretion and intestinal-barrier passage (Tenenbaum et al., 2023). The two digested materials showed similar DPP-IV inhibition in the reported comparisons, while the prehydrolysed material had a different peptide distribution and stimulated greater GLP-1 secretion under the tested conditions. Holding the material comparison within one study identifies what a broad “antidiabetic” prediction cannot specify on its own: whether the target response is enzyme inhibition, a cellular outcome or another property of the digested material.

## 5.3 Processing, digestion and food matrices require their own evidence

Predicted release, measured occurrence and retained activity characterize different properties of a candidate in food. Virtual proteolysis identifies sequences compatible with an assumed cleavage procedure. Kumagai et al. identified EVYR as a DPP-IV inhibitor and quantified it in pepsin-trypsin digestion products from dulse powder and extracted protein (Kumagai et al., 2026). The measured peptide contents differ between preparations, connecting candidate identity with a specific processing context. A rice-bran study combined peptide identification, synthesis, cell-based inhibition and digestion-stability observations (Zeng et al., 2026). These experiments put actual occurrence and post-processing behaviour beside predicted release. A claim about peptides produced by digestion therefore depends on the measured material and a specified digestion protocol, including its phases, enzymes, pH and duration.

Digestion can transform the candidate itself. Nongonierma and FitzGerald distinguish peptides that lose activity after DPP-IV cleavage from prodrug-type material that yields more active products (Nongonierma and FitzGerald, 2014). The biological question can therefore shift from persistence of the parent sequence to the identities and activity of its products. Reviews of peptide barrier prediction make a related distinction between a molecular property and direct evidence for passage through a barrier (An et al., 2026). For food screening, measuring the digest, identifying relevant products and attributing activity to them are complementary steps. Their order is set by the claim: predicted stability concerns likely cleavage, a digest assay concerns the mixture's response, and product-level attribution concerns the molecules responsible for that response.

Comparing predicted cleavage with measured products tests virtual digestion more directly than a predicted cleavage map alone. Turalić et al. examined five previously identified milk-derived DPP-IV inhibitory peptides and an additional cleavage-site dataset against in vitro digestion (Turalić et al., 2025). The peptides were largely stable during the specified three-hour incubations, although several predicted cleavage events were absent from the experiments. This comparison evaluates cleavage prediction and parent-sequence persistence; the earlier inhibition assays supply a different observation about activity. A virtual-digestion pipeline thus has an observable product-level check before a processed sample's effect is attributed to a selected peptide.

An application experiment measures the peptide's added effect against the processed food itself. Yao et al. incorporated linear LQAFEPLR and a cyclic variant into biscuits, examined heating and simulated digestion, and measured α-glucosidase inhibition and starch-digestion behaviour (Yao et al., 2026). Control biscuit digesta already showed 29.2% α-glucosidase inhibition. Fortified digesta showed 39.3% for the linear and 46.9% for the cyclic peptide; only the cyclic comparison was statistically significant against the control for that endpoint. The matched biscuit identifies a response above matrix background. This is an α-glucosidase experiment, not a DPP-IV test; its relevant negative comparator is a processing condition without added peptide rather than a sequence assigned zero in a predictor's training file.

# 6. Interpreting negative evidence for a defined screening use

## 6.1 Match the comparison to the intended use

The review's three analytical levels yield different practical judgments (Table 4). Seven F665 versions support comparisons of algorithms on a shared labelled cohort, but their common test members provide one reference population, not seven independent populations. A5's assays measure the activity of tested molecules; estimating the yield or errors of its selection rule requires outcomes from candidates outside the selected group. Neither the F665 label audit nor a binary activity score estimates numerical potency or an added food effect; the reviewed concentration-response and matched-matrix experiments address those claims directly. Alaei et al. (2026) likewise distinguish model validation from evidence for food-processing claims, while DOME, REFORMS and FAIR supply practical expectations for documented data, decisions and access (Walsh et al., 2021; Kapoor et al., 2024; Wilkinson et al., 2016). Table 4 states what each observed comparison establishes and what remains to be measured.

## 6.2 Priorities that follow from the evidence

For a food researcher selecting a prediction tool, the useful record is its negative-set version, the candidates it was compared against and the point at which test outcomes could influence a decision. The F665 length and milk-reference contrasts make candidate length and possible precursor matches relevant to that comparison; neither establishes whether the predictor uses those properties. The A4 and B1 code paths show why a reported test file alone cannot describe all selection decisions. To assess a shortlist for synthesis, experiments on sampled low-priority as well as high-priority peptides would measure how well the ranking separates candidates in the intended population. Safety and additional activity are distinct endpoints: label noise has also been reported in a peptide-toxicity preprint, while food-peptide reviews discuss separate toxicity testing and functional conditions (Almeida et al., 2026, preprint; X. Liu et al., 2026; Luo et al., 2027).

For dataset assembly, an assigned zero should retain its underlying evidence: an absent annotation, another recorded function, a generation rule or measured non-response with endpoint and assay conditions. Food-peptide resources retain parts of that context, including candidate precursors, functional records and fermentation information (Minkiewicz et al., 2019; Nielsen et al., 2017; Li et al., 2018; Chaudhary et al., 2021; Qin et al., 2022). The F665/Bilingua comparison adds a specific version record: the seven conflicting identities removed by re-curation and the three new benchmark negatives should be identifiable when interpreting scores across the two datasets. This documents the compared labels without assuming that the revised score changed for any particular reason.

For model comparison, target, test members, metric and selection procedure jointly define the reported score. The ADP-Hybrid reconstruction examines homology-aware partitions; simple-feature peptide baselines make source- and length-aware evaluations informative comparisons within a specified dataset (Islam et al., 2026, preprint; Adamczyk et al., 2026; Whalen et al., 2022). Checkpoint, threshold and ensemble choices must also be assessed against the data used to make them, as the A4 and B1 code paths illustrate (Cawley and Talbot, 2010). For food claims, the relevant observation changes with the intended outcome: a ranking calls for measured responses across sampled predictions, numerical potency for concentration-response errors, digestion for identified products, and added ingredient effect for a matched processed matrix (Nongonierma and FitzGerald, 2016; Zan et al., 2023; Kumagai et al., 2026; Yao et al., 2026; Liang et al., 2025).

## 6.3 Scope of the synthesis and unresolved work

The audit is based on a purposive study sample and inspected file versions, so it does not estimate how common a practice is across the field or how any score would change on retraining. H4's test members, A7's paper-to-repository mapping and A12's full published corpus remain unresolved; A10's reported download could not be accessed at the audit cutoff. The F665 milk-protein annotation is an exact reference-sequence match to six selected precursors and does not establish actual origin or activity. File-level differences beyond negative completeness are recorded in Supplementary Table S6, including A4's 531 positive training records and B1's 839 positive benchmark records representing 838 identities.

The next informative comparisons are correspondingly specific. Holding positives and model selection fixed while changing the negative sampling rule tests the role of construction in a model comparison; assaying sampled high- and low-priority candidates tests selection in its target population; comparing a candidate in solution, after digestion and within a matched food matrix tests different properties of its intended use. These comparisons address distinct uncertainties and can be pursued independently. The audited versions provide concrete label sets for the first design, while the reviewed experiments show how to measure the outcomes needed for the other two (Sidorczuk et al., 2022; Guan et al., 2023; Kumagai et al., 2026; Yao et al., 2026).

# 7. Conclusions

Negative labels drawn from missing annotations, other functions, construction rules and measured non-responses define different benchmark targets. Across the inspected versions, seven predictors share the same F665 negative members and split; seven identities have opposite labels, match a bovine beta-casein reference sequence and are absent from one reported re-curation. In another reference set, earlier test members acquire later development or external-test roles. Released implementations show how evaluation data can also enter checkpoint and method selection. Published food experiments answer different questions through measured candidate responses, defined assays, digestion products and matched-matrix effects. These observations allow each kind of claim to be assessed against the evidence that directly supports it, without treating a benchmark score as an experimental food outcome.
